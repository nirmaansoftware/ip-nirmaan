# Design for Test (Milestone 25, DFT part)

Status: design for the owner's review, implemented on branch `m25/dft`. This is
the DFT part of Stage 6 of `docs/ROADMAP.md`; physical design and firmware are
built in parallel. Prose here is free of em and en dashes per the standing style
law.

M21 made lint, simulation, synthesis, and formal real through open-source EDA.
M23 seated models in design and gated RTL on real runs before review. M25 adds
design for test on the same two foundations: scan insertion, testability rules,
and a scan chain simulation, each a real run of Yosys or Icarus Verilog, and a
DFT stage whose netlist reaches review only after the rules and the chain
simulation passed on it.

There is no Fault and no commercial DFT tool here. Everything real runs on
Yosys plus simulation, and works on Yosys 0.33 (what Ubuntu 24.04 ships, and CI
runs) as well as current Yosys.

---

## 1. The laws

M21's and M23's laws hold unchanged. In particular:

1. **Never fake work.** Each DFT tool is a registered M21 backend. When an
   executable is missing, the broker refuses and says which one; nothing is
   recorded as a run. A design that fails a rule, or a chain that does not
   shift, is a recorded run with `succeeded=False`.
2. **Data over code.** The DFT seat, its skill, its tools, and its stage are
   records in `company/`. The runtime, engine, and policy do not name DFT.
3. **Registries.** A new testability rule is `register_rule(DftRule(...))`; a
   new DFT tool backend is `register_backend(Backend(...))`. Neither needs a
   change to `dft.py` or the core.

## 2. Scan architecture: mux-D, one chain

The simplest structure that makes every flop controllable and observable: each
D flip-flop gets a 2:1 multiplexer in front of its D input.

```
             scan_en
                |
   D ------ A |\|
               | |---- D'  [flop] ---- Q ---+---> functional logic
   SI ----- B |/                            |
                                            +---> SI of the next flop
```

With `scan_en` low, the flop loads its functional D (capture). With `scan_en`
high, it loads the previous flop's Q (shift). The first flop's SI is the new
input `scan_in`; the last flop's Q is the new output `scan_out`.

**How it is built** (`dft.scan_insert`, backend `yosys-scan`, three steps):

| Step | What runs | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Prepare | Yosys: `read_verilog`, `hierarchy -check`, `synth -flatten`, `dffunmap`, `write_json` | `design.json`: a flat netlist of Yosys gate cells and plain flops |
| 2. Stitch | `dft_scan.py stitch`: each `$_DFF_*` cell becomes a `$__NIRMAAN_SCAN_DFF_*` cell with `SE` and `SI` pins; `SI` of flop k is Q of flop k-1 | `stitched.json`, `chain.json` |
| 3. Expand | Yosys: `read_json`, `techmap -map scan_map.v` (each scan cell becomes a `$_MUX_` in front of the original flop), `check -assert`, `write_verilog` | `scan.v` (the scan netlist) |

`dffunmap` turns enable and synchronous reset flops into plain flops with the
logic in front, so every flop left is `$_DFF_[PN]_` or, with an asynchronous
reset or set, `$_DFF_[PN][PN][01]_`, and one techmap template per type covers
them all. `scan_map.v` is generated by `dft_scan.techmap_file()`.

**Chain order** is the natural order of the net each flop drives
(`count[0]`, `count[1]`, ...), so a chain report reads like the RTL.

**What insertion refuses** (a recorded failed run, with the reason): a design
with no flops; a latch or other sequential cell with no mux-D equivalent; more
than one clock (or both edges), since one chain serves one clock; a clock that
is not a module input; a top module that already has a `scan_en`, `scan_in`, or
`scan_out` port.

**The chain report** (`chain.json`, also in the run's result metrics): length,
clock port and edge, the three scan ports, and the order (position, flop, cell,
original type).

## 3. Testability rules (`dft.check`)

Backend `yosys-dft`: Yosys synthesizes the design exactly as step 1 above and
writes the netlist; every registered rule is then evaluated over that netlist.
Each violation is a `Diagnostic` whose `code` is the rule's ID, so the run's
`EdaResult` says which rule failed and where.

| Rule | Violation |
|---|---|
| `no-latches` | a latch (`$_DLATCH_*`, `$_SR_*`): it cannot be scanned |
| `no-combinational-loops` | a strongly connected group of gate cells |
| `scannable-flops` | a flop with no mux-D equivalent (set and reset both, async load) |
| `clock-from-input` | a flop clocked by anything but a module input: a gated clock, a divided clock, a constant |
| `reset-from-input` | an asynchronous reset or set driven by logic rather than a module input |
| `one-clock-domain` | flops on more than one clock or edge |
| `recognized-cells` | a cell the checker cannot evaluate (a black box, an unmapped cell) |
| `scan-chain-complete` | (only when the top has the three scan ports) a flop not on the chain from `scan_in` to `scan_out`, a fork, or `scan_out` not the last flop |

A flop with no reset is not a violation: scan loads its state. A synchronous
reset is data, not a reset, for these rules.

**The chain rule is functional, not structural.** Synthesis may restructure the
scan multiplexers, so the rule does not look for `$_MUX_` cells. It gives every
module input and flop output a random 64-bit word, holds `scan_en` high and
asynchronous resets inactive, and evaluates each flop's D over the netlist's
gates (64 simulations at once). A flop is on the chain when its D equals
exactly the word of `scan_in` or of one other flop; the chain is then followed
from `scan_in`. A chance match has probability 2^-64. A combinational loop is
reported, not evaluated.

**Registry.** `DftRule(id, description, check, applies=None)`; `check` is a
function of the `Design` (the parsed netlist) returning violation messages.
`register_rule` and `unregister_rule` are the extension point.

## 4. Proving the chain works (`dft.scan_sim`)

Backend `icarus-scan`, four steps: Yosys analyzes the scan netlist (as above);
`dft_scan.py testbench` traces the chain and writes a self-checking testbench;
Icarus compiles the scan netlist with it; `vvp` runs it.

The testbench is generic, generated from the netlist:

1. **Shift.** With `scan_en` high, 2L known bits go in at `scan_in` (L is the
   chain length). From cycle L on, `scan_out` before each clock edge must be
   the bit shifted in L cycles earlier.
2. **Capture.** A known state is shifted in (chain flop k holds state bit k),
   the primary inputs are held at known values (asynchronous resets inactive),
   and one clock with `scan_en` low captures the functional next state. It is
   shifted out and compared, bit by bit, with the expected state.

**Where the expected capture comes from.** The netlist's next-state function,
evaluated by the same gate evaluator the chain rule uses, over what Yosys
synthesized from the scan netlist. So the simulation cross-checks two
independent readings of one netlist: Icarus's event-driven simulation of the
Verilog, and the evaluation of Yosys's gate netlist. A mismatch in either the
shift or the capture is a `$error`, so the run fails exactly as an M21
simulation does.

If the chain is broken (a flop left off, a fork), there is no definite state to
capture, and the testbench step refuses with the chain problems; the run is
recorded as failed before any simulation.

The pass summary: `scan chain passed: 4 flops, 8 bits shifted through, one
capture checked, 0 mismatches`.

## 5. The DFT seat

All data; the runtime does not name DFT.

| Record | Change |
|---|---|
| Tools (`company/tools.py`) | `dft.scan_insert`, `dft.check`, `dft.scan_sim`, all `AVAILABLE` (real backends). `dft.run` (ATPG, MBIST) stays `CONTRACT_ONLY`. |
| Capability | `dft.insert` (existing), producing `dft_netlist` |
| Skills | `scan_design` (provides `dft.insert`) gains the three tools; `dft_verification` gains `dft.check` and `dft.scan_sim` |
| Unit | the existing DFT Engineering team (`implementation.dft.engineering`), whose Scan practice holds `scan_design` and whose team skill `dft_architecture` provides `dft.review` |
| Workflow | `block-design` gains a `dft` stage, "Scan insertion", after `rtl-implementation`, planned only when the request asks for test (the existing `dft` feature: "DFT", "scan chain", "ATPG", "MBIST") |

The stage's evidence follows the M23 pattern:

```python
st("dft", "Scan insertion", "Implementation", "dft.insert",
   depends_on=("rtl-implementation",), when=when("dft"), skills=("scan_design",), review=rv("dft.review"),
   outputs=("dft_netlist",),
   evidence=(REVIEWED,
             checked("Testability rules hold and every flop is on the chain", "dft.check",
                     FileInput(param="sources", kinds=("dft_netlist",)),
                     FileInput(param="top", kinds=("dft_netlist",), entry=True)),
             checked("The scan chain shifts and captures in simulation", "dft.scan_sim", ...)))
```

`checked(...)` marks both requirements `before_review`, so the M23 policy check
`evidence-before-review` refuses to submit a scan netlist until a passing
`dft.check` run and a passing `dft.scan_sim` run cover that exact file.

"Create an AXI4-Lite register block." plans exactly as before; "Create a 4-bit
wrapping counter with scan chains." plans the same four stages plus `dft`.

**Who fills the seat.** A DFT engineer runs `dft.scan_insert` on the approved
RTL, then `dft.check` and `dft.scan_sim` on the netlist it wrote, records the
runs as evidence, and submits the netlist (with its location). An M23 model
seat meets the same gate, since the stage is ordinary data, but a model that
writes a netlist by hand is not the intended use: see Deferred.

| To add | Do this | Core changes |
|---|---|---|

Crown jewel `test_a_new_dft_rule_needs_no_core_changes`: a rule the core has
never seen ("every flop has an asynchronous reset") is registered in the test,
fails the counter for real, and passes once removed.

## 7. Laws, each pinned by a test (`tests/test_nirmaan_dft.py`)

1. Scan insertion runs for real on `counter.v` and `axi4_lite_regs.v`: every
   flop on one chain, the three ports on the top, a chain report.
2. The chain shift and capture test passes in Icarus on both scan netlists.
3. A broken chain (a flop with no scan mux) fails `dft.check` with the flop
   named, and fails `dft.scan_sim`; both are recorded failed runs.
4. A latch, a gated clock, a divided clock, and a combinational loop are each
   reported by their rule on a small bad fixture, and scan insertion refuses the
   design.
5. With Yosys absent, all three tools are refused with the reason; nothing is
   recorded.
6. The stitcher, the chain tracer, and the rules are tested on hand-written
   netlists with no tool installed.
7. The DFT stage is planned only when test is asked for, and its netlist cannot
   go to review until real rule checks and a real chain simulation passed on it.
8. Crown jewel: a new rule needs no core changes.
9. The import laws hold (`test_nirmaan_architecture.py`).

## 8. Deferred

M27 (`docs/DFT_ADVANCED.md`) delivers ATPG with fault grading, multiple chains
grouped by clock domain, and March C- MBIST.

* **ATPG and fault coverage.** Needs a fault simulator; `dft.run` stays a
  contract. Stuck-at grading with Yosys plus simulation is possible and is the
  natural next step.
* **Multiple chains and clock domains.** One chain per clock and edge, with
  lockup latches between domains. Today a second clock is refused.
* **Chain balancing and compression**, MBIST, LBIST, JTAG boundary scan.
* **A tool-producing seat.** M23 records files a model writes; a DFT seat run
  by a model should instead record the netlist that `dft.scan_insert` wrote.
  That is a runtime feature (files produced by a tool run), not DFT data.
* **A Verilator backend for `dft.scan_sim`.** One more `register_backend`.
* The `new-ip` workflow's `dft` stage still takes only a review; giving it the
  same before-review checks is a data edit once its upstream stages record
  located RTL files.
